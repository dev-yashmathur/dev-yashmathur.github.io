Classic Games Hub · by Yash Mathur

Old games, new twists, and someone to play them with.

I kept seeing clever twists on childhood games on social media, and had nobody around to play them with and nowhere online to try them. So I built Classic Games Hub: five takes on tic-tac-toe you can play in the browser with a friend, against the computer, or in an online room.

Why it exists

Plain tic-tac-toe almost always ends in a draw. The twists keep the simple rules and the old feeling, but you no longer know how it ends, which makes it worth playing again.

The games

Five games on one wooden board

Classic & Anti

The one you know, and the one that flips it

Classic is the 3×3 game from every school notebook: get three in a row. Anti uses the same board with the goal turned upside down: if you make three of your own in a row, you lose.

  • Same board, opposite goals
  • Anti warns you about moves that would sink you

Stack

Size matters

Each player gets six pieces: two large, two medium and two small. A bigger piece can cover a smaller one, even your opponent’s, and only the pieces on top count towards three in a row.

  • Two large, two medium, two small each
  • Cover a smaller piece to take the square

Play Stack on Classic Games Hub →

Vanishing

Only three pieces each, so nothing stays put

You can only have three pieces on the board. Place a fourth and your oldest one disappears. The piece that goes next turns grey, so you can see the board changing under you. With six pieces at most on nine squares, the board never fills up.

  • Three pieces each, at most
  • Your oldest piece vanishes when you place a new one

Play Vanishing on Classic Games Hub →

Ultimate

Nine games inside one

The board is nine small boards in a 3×3 grid. Where you play inside a small board decides which small board your opponent has to play in next. Win a small board to claim it, and claim three in a row to win.

  • Every move picks your opponent’s next board
  • If that board is finished, they can play anywhere

How I built it · 2024

A side project that started with nobody to play with

The twists I saw online looked like the perfect mix: simple, exciting, and still true to the games I grew up with. I couldn’t find a single place to play them with friends or strangers, so I made one.

  • React
  • Next.js
  • Firebase
  • Vercel
  • SEO
  1. X

    Ideation and validation

    I found the idea in social media trends, then checked it with friends and looked at how much people engaged with similar games before writing any code.

  2. O

    Design and development

    I gave it a warm, playful, slightly 3D wooden look, wrote the game logic in React and Next.js, and used Firebase for live multiplayer so there was no server to run.

  3. X

    Testing and launch

    Local two-player and computer modes came first, then online rooms. It deploys to Vercel, and I keep an eye on its search metrics to help people find it.

Questions

What is Classic Games Hub?

Classic Games Hub is a free web app for playing modern twists on childhood games in the browser. Right now it has five takes on tic-tac-toe: Classic, Anti, Stack, Vanishing and Ultimate. You play it at classicgameshub.com.

Who made Classic Games Hub?

Yash Mathur, a product engineer. He designed and built it himself in 2024, after seeing these game twists on social media and finding nowhere online to play them with other people.

Is Classic Games Hub free? Do I need an account?

It is free and runs in your browser, with nothing to download. You can play as a guest; signing in only shows your name to the people you play with online.

Can I play Classic Games Hub with friends?

Yes. You can pass and play with a friend on one device, play against the computer, or create an online room and share its code so a friend can join from their own device.

How was Classic Games Hub built?

The games are written in React and Next.js. Live multiplayer runs on Firebase, so there is no game server to manage, and the site is deployed on Vercel.

Where do I play Classic Games Hub?

At classicgameshub.com. This page is the story behind it; the games themselves, their rules and their FAQs all live on the Classic Games Hub site.
